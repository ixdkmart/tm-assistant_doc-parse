Soiled & Damaged Process

Soiled and Damaged (S&D) merchandise, are products that are no longer saleable at their full price due to either:

• The product has missing parts.
• The product is no longer a full set.
• The product was a multi pack, however one or more products are missing.

Soiled and damaged merchandise adjustments create large volumes of shrinkage in stores, and it's important that each soiled and damaged product is reviewed for potential resale, to reduce shrink. A dedicated box or elegant trolley should be placed with the RLO processing area specifically for soiled and damaged merchandise, so it can be reviewed and processed each day.

Please Note: NZ Stores do not have an RLO option. If items cannot be sold items must be processed through the PDT device under NZ RLO and items are then to be disposed of with manager approval.

Products that Cannot be Sold Through Soiled & Damaged
✕ End of range/ oddments apparel.
✕ Apparel that is stained or damaged e.g., a tear in the material, chemical marks, sweat, body fluids/markings or used.
✕ Clearance merchandise.
✕ Returned undergarments e.g., underwear, lingerie, swimwear.
✕ Faulty, returned or display electrical products: Electrical merchandise cannot be tested or markdown under soiled and damaged. This includes display products, as we cannot guarantee that the electrical product is in full and safe working order.
✕ Any items missing the safety and/or compliance warning information labels e.g., aquatic items without packaging, bike helmets without the extra padding.
✕ Any items containing sharp or dangerous parts/substances, including broken glass.
✕ Items deemed unsafe to sell as they have missing parts or parts are damaged e.g., a bike has wheel nuts missing, a highchair has straps missing, scooter is missing brakes.
✕ Items that breach health and safety regulations e.g., open cosmetics, open grocery items.
✕ Toys where the damage poses a choking hazard or risk of injury.

How to Manage & Process Soiled & Damaged Merchandise

Step | Action
Step 1 | The service desk, register areas and other collection points for returns and S&D merchandise must be checked throughout the day and collected.
Info | At the end of each day Service Desk Team Members and Register Operators should check their areas for soiled and damaged merchandise and place into the soiled and damaged dedicated RLO processing area, so each product can be reviewed and processed as per the S&D process.
Step 2 | Determine if the S&D merchandise is suitable for any of the options below using the RLO Assessment function via a PDT device:
1. Reverse Logistics Operation (RLO), to return the product from the store to a central returns centre where it is consolidated and returned to suppliers and a small reimbursement is provided.
2. Resale (markdown), ensuring the product does not fall under any of the 'Soiled and Damaged Merchandise that Cannot be Sold Criteria' listed in this guide.
3. Items that cannot be returned via RLO or markdown for resale
Step 3 | If the S&D merchandise is faulty and cannot be marked down for resale, process the product as per the standard RLO process (Refer to the RLO Process Guide for instructions) If the S&D merchandise can be markdown for resale, proceed to step 5.

How to Markdown Soiled & Damaged Products

Step 1
If the item can be markdown and sold and does not fall under any of the 'Soiled and Damaged Merchandise that Cannot be Sold Criteria' markdown the products as a percentage off as per below:

S&D Markdown | Percentage Off
The first S&D markdown | 50% off the original sell price
The second S&D markdown | 50% off the previous markdown price
Additional S&D markdown* | 50% off the previous markdown price

*Only for products where the previous markdown price is $10 or greater.

Info
If products do not sell after the third and final markdown:
• AUS stores - Use the RLO assessment via a PDT device to see if the item can be added to claimable or salvage.
• NZ Stores - Write off under RLO via PDT device.

Step 2
When marking down the price of soiled and damaged merchandise, prices should be rounded up to the nearest 50 cent denomination. Here are some examples:
$ 5.01 ✗    $ 5.00 ✓
$ 10.51 ✗   $ 10.50 ✓
$ 6.25 ✗    $ 6.50 ✓

Step 3
When items are mark down in price under soiled and damaged, they should be placed on show as per the schedule below. If a soiled and damaged marked down merchandise items does not sell as per the schedule, items should be reviewed by the RLO Team Member to apply any further markdowns (if applicable).

S&D Markdown | Number of Days on Show
The first S&D markdown | 2 Days
The second S&D markdown | 2 Days
Additional S&D markdown* | 2 Days
Final Markdown | Write off under Soiled & Damaged

Location & Presentation of Clearance Products on the Shop Floor

When merchandise is identified as Soiled and Damaged (S&D), however can be sold at a reduced price, it's important that it is displayed on the shop floor as outlined below, so not to detract from the overall in store customer experience.

Setup | Action

Label
- Soiled and damaged products must be displayed with a Soiled & Damaged label including why the item has been marked down. Team members must not write on the label.

Location
- All S&D merchandise for sale must be displayed on an end and in a consistent, and non-conspicuous location within the store e.g., back aisle of the store, near the service desk area.
- All S&D merchandise MUST NOT be placed in a trolley or H-Cage, in a walkway, at the front of the store, or front of a department or in the toy or nursery departments.

Presentation
The S&D clearance ends must always be maintained in line with Kmart presentation standards which includes:
- Any rubbish and empty packets must be removed and regularly processes.
- S&D merchandise must be kept tidy on a regular basis, and any unsaleable stock removed.
- Decks, pallets or bunks are not to be used to merchandise the S&D stock.

Clearance Signage
No extra signage, other than the clearance header card inserts, should be displayed on the soiled & damaged product end. Replacement header cards can be ordered through Bluestar.

Big & Bulky Merchandise
Big and bulky items such as furniture that are soiled and damaged, must not be displayed on or near the S&D clearance trolley, and should be displayed in their home location, as they are more likely to sell from this location.

High Risk Merchandise
High valued S&D merchandise of high risk that would normally have Merchandise Protection Equipment (MPE) applied, must be merchandised with the MPE e.g., Lego, fishing, camping etc.
For high-risk merchandise that is displayed with empty boxes/cards as a silent salesman, items must be authorised by a manager prior to marking down, and displayed xxx

Soiled & Damaged Label Standards - What Good Looks Like

Do's:
- Ensure the label is placed neatly on the product so information can be read.
- Loose products and items must be placed into a plastic warning bag.

Don'ts:
- Don't write additional information on the labels.
- Do not use as a markdown for clearance products.
- The product must be in a saleable condition (refer to page 1 of this guide). If you are unsure discuss with your line manager.

Do's:

Safety
When managing soiled and damaged stock, Managers and Team Members must ensure that:
• All Managers and Team Members have completed the manual handling training and moving stock SWP's within the current year.
• Storage and display of soiled and damaged merchandise for resale are as per the safety care manuals.
• All heavy and awkward merchandise is a 2-person lift.
• That any equipment required for the moving of merchandise is in good working order e.g., flatbed trolleys.
• Heavy items are stored in the stockroom correctly with allowance for clear access to heavy items.

For further information refer to the Safety Care manuals within your store.

Repackaging
All Soiled and damaged products must be repackaged using bags available to order through Bluestar. These bags have warning messages printed on the bag with the following messaging:

What If Scenarios

Below are some scenarios that you will frequently come across when processing S&D products, and guidelines on how to manage them in line with the process outlined in this guide. These scenarios will help you effectively make decisions on products that can and cannot be marked down for S&D clearance ends for re-sale.

Scenario | Response
---------|----------
Can an electrical item that has been returned as a change of mind be re-sold? | It can if; the warning labels and instruction booklets are not missing, and the item has not been used or listed as faulty.
If an item still has its seal sticker on it, and it has not been opened, can it be re-sold? | Yes, any items clearly not opened should be re-sold.
Can Manchester be re-sold? | Yes, Manchester should be treated the same as clothing, and re-sold as long as it is not soiled. Repackaging using the plastic bags with the suffocation warning label.
Can items with marks, scratches, dents and missing parts be re-sold? | Yes, just make sure it is listed on the yellow S&D label, so the customer is aware when making a purchase decision.
I've found an item e.g., blanket in the home location without packaging. The packing has been left on the floor. Should this go into S&D clearance? | No, you should apply the packaging onto the item and place back onto the shelf (as long at the item hasn't been damaged). ***Repackaging is not clearance and should be placed back into the home location
I have a big item that can be re-sold in the S&D Clearance end, but it's too big to go there, what should I do? | Talk to your manager to discuss location options such as placing back into the home location with the yellow S&D label and mark down on it.
