RLO Process Guide

Reverse Logistics Operation (RLO) are branded products customers have purchased and returned to stores that are not of resaleable condition. These products are processed through a PDT device using the RLO application to identify if the items are:

• Claimable - where suppliers provide Kmart with a claim for the goods or
• Salvage - the items cannot be claimed but RLO reimburse Kmart for 7% of the cost of the goods

Salvage RLO also incurs an additional cost of freight, so it's important that stores carefully review each product to ensure the correct action is taken to minimise these costs, whilst also ensuring that minimal stock is applied to shrink. The RLO and returns process allows stores to:

• Capture the required information for the reasons customers return products.
• Utilising the Soiled and Damaged (S&D) process to try and sell items at a discounted price.
• Achieve significant freight savings and an increased salvage income from the RLO vendor.

Customer Returns

It's important that when customers return products, Team Members provide clear reasons for the returns so that:

• Items can be reviewed and if resaleable can be markdown under soiled and damaged'
• Assists Kmart merchandise teams to work with suppliers to improve the durability and quality of products

It's important that the correct return reason is applied to all returned products to ensure only saleable products are sold at a marked down price under soiled and damaged, with minimal impact to the stores shrink, and ensuring the product is safe to resell as a marked down, soiled and damaged product.

Please Note:
Electrical Products that are returned are not to be tested, as electrical exposure is a critical risk. These items must be processed through RLO and not to be resold.

Faulty Return Reasons

Reason | Description
------|-------------
Shrunk when washed | The item has shrunk or changed shape after being washed (includes clothing, bedding, Manchester and napery).
Colour loss/staining | The item has lost colour or stained other items as a result of being washed (includes clothing, bedding, Manchester and napery).
Wrong shape/fit | The item is distorted in some way e.g., one sleeve is shorter than the other, stitching is incorrect.
Damage/missing part | The item has superficial damage such as scratches, dents, chips missing parts e.g., Lego piece, part of a kitchen set etc.).
Broke during use | The item was working when removed from the packaging but failed after being used by the customer.
Not working | The item did not work at all when removed from the packaging.
Electrical fault | Any electrical item fault when switched on including odour or sound, tripping of the safety switch etc. All electrical items that have caused a safety incident or risk or has resulted in property damage etc. must be returned using the existing 'Compensation Damages claim or 'Injury' options.

Faulty Returns Dockets

All faulty customer returns will automatically produce a 'Soiled and Damaged' docket from the register printer for each faulty item returned, except for items returns with a quantity key. In these instances, only one docket will be produced for the items.

It is recommended that all customer returns are processed item by item so that an individual soiled and damaged docket is printed for each item. Each Soiled and Damaged docket contains the following information:

• APN
• Description
• Refunded price
• Return reason
• Multi format barcode

Customer Return Process

Step | Action
-----|--------
Step 1 | Process the customer return through POS and apply the correct option (change of mind or faulty), with the applicable reasons (refer to change of mind reason page 1 and faulty reasons page 2 of this guide).
Info | You may need to ask the customer questions to determine the appropriate return reason.
Step 2 | Place the returned item/s into the return's tub/cage.
Step 3 | The returns docket will print from the POS printer for any faulty returns. Tape the docket to the faulty item/s.
Info | Avoid applying sticky tape to the printed area of the returns docket as this can cause the ink to fade.
Step 4 | Throughout and at the end of each day, take the faulty items to the RLO area in the receiving area of the store for processing.
Step 5 | Each day review the faulty items.
Info | Electrical products are not to be tested or re-sold.

RLO Process Guide
Back of House Process

All items placed within the RLO area must be reviewed daily, using the RLO functions located under the Store > Receiving & Dispatch option on a PDT device. This application helps determine the most profitable way to manage each item. Refer to the RLO Processing on PDT (With Markdowns) process for instructions.

Stores should also reference the equipment process (captive and non-captive) stores when managing equipment such as pallets:
• Equipment Control Process (Captive Stores) AUS
• Equipment Control Process (Non-Captive Stores) AUS
• Equipment Control Process NZ

There should be 2 areas in your back of house to separate items into RLO 'Claimable' or 'Salvage' (Non-Claimable). This area should be setup in a space that is accessible to Team Members who process RLO/Service Desk, and has enough room to safely add items to, but can also be easily accessed when full and ready for collection.

Stores:

Store should use pallets for claimable items, which should be stacked neatly onto a pallet. Salvage items should be placed into a jumbo box that is placed on top of a pallet, ensuring everything is stacked neatly, filling (but not overflowing) in the box.

K Hubs:

Due to the size of a K Hub store, a pallet may not be an option as it takes up too much room. In this instance stores can use boxes, making sure that there is a box for claimable and a box for salvage (these must not be mixed). Stores who use boxes, should keep boxes aside for this use, and can use any boxes for claimable and salvage RLO.
Once the boxes are full, they can be dispatched and placed onto an empty equipment that is returned to a DC or a hanger box.

Please Note: These boxes must not be heavier than 12kg, so be mindful as you place stock into the boxes to ensure this weight is not exceeded

Is returned to your local DC and is exported overseas.

Salvage (non-claimable) items are to be placed into a jumbo box. A single SCM is required for the claimable pallet (apply this to the box so all Team Members know what this area is for).

RLO Assessment
The 'RLO Assessment' function on a PDT device is used to scan and check all soiled and damaged items to determine the most suitable action for the highest profitable outcome. The RLO assessment function on the PDT will step through various criteria to determine the most appropriate outcome including:

• If the item is suitable to be marked down and sold on the Soiled and Damaged clearance end
• Whether the item is claimable against the supplier
• The cost price of each item compares with the overall cost to process and transport back to RLO
• Whether the item is on withdrawal or recall

All soiled and damaged items must be scanned using the RLO Assessment function. If an item is completely destroyed and no longer of any value do not scan through the RLO assessment function, and process as a normal soiled and damaged write off with manager approval.

These instructions provided on the PDT must be followed, to ensure the merchandise is managed correctly with the highest profitable outcome.

Build & Dispatch RLO Pallets (Stores)

RLO Type | Build & Dispatch Pallet Process

Sale/Return & Recall Merchandise
1. Create the relevant SCMs
2. Pack into cartons as required
3. Create a shipment and add the relevant SCMs to the shipment
4. Dispatch the shipment

These cartons will be sent to RLO in Melbourne VIC and are added to claimable pallets.

Claimable Pallets
In most instances claimable pallets will consist of one SCM, created using the RLO Assessment function on a PDT device. This will contain all claimable soiled and damaged or faulty items scanned.

1. Add these items to the claimable pallet, keeping them together where possible.
2. Ensure the claimable component is dispatched with the RLO dispatch function on a PDT device. This SCM cannot be added to a shipment.
3. Ensure the SCMs are shipped through the PDT using the shipment functions.

All salvage merchandise must be packed into a jumbo box, which should sit on a standard pallet. Stores are not required to pack items into cartons when placing in the jumbo box, except where it is believed that the item is fragile and will sustain further damaged during transport.

Salvage Pallets
1. Ensure the SCM dispatched with the RLO dispatch function. This SCM cannot be added to a shipment.
2. Ensure the cutaway flaps are taped to prevent them from opening.
3. Ensure the SCM has been placed on the box.
4. Ensure the pallet has been filled to the top of the box. Boxes must never be under filled or overfilled, as these are exported directly from the DC and will not stack in the container correctly.
5. Place larger items onto the additional permitted bulky salvage pallet.

Place a small amount of shrink wrap around the lid, to keep it in the box when moved to the DC.
