## Page 2

Introduction to Sequence

Once the load has been decanted, the next step is to fill it onto the shopfloor. This process is called sequence, and it consists of four tasks that are designed to efficiently replenish our stores.

Each task is to be completed as a team before moving on to the next task.

[Circular diagram showing four connected steps of Sequence:]
- Load Fill
- Pack Away
- Back Fill
- Shopkeeping

The Benefits of Sequence

• Safe shopfloor filling principles.

• Improves speed to shelf, with 24hour load turnaround.

• Improved productivity.

• Provides a structured and measurable process with milestones.

• Balances workload.

• Provides the leadership team tools to drive pace and complete workload.

• Creates a team environment.

• Provides role clarity for the team.

All team members to complete the mandatory safety training modules.

5s Principles

5s is a set of 5 principles designed to improve organisation cleanliness and efficiency in the workplace.

The 5S process involves assessing everything in the space, removing what is unnecessary, organising things logically, adding visual cues for the team, keeping the area clean, and ensuring this is sustained.

By following these 5S principals and embedding them as part of your stores culture you will:
• Improve productivity
• Improve team member morale
• Reduce costs
• Improved safety performance

5S Lean Workplace:
1. Sort - Keeping only what is necessary and discard everything else
2. Set In Order - Arranging and labelling necessary items for easy use and return
3. Shine - Keeping everything swept and clean
4. Standardise - The state that exists when the first 3 S's are properly maintained
5. Sustain - Making a habit of properly maintaining correct procedures

Waste

Waste refers to any activity or action that does not add value to the customer or the business.

In retail, waste can manifest in several different ways such as long wait times, inefficient or unnecessary processes, or actions that we take that decreases our productivity and makes our roles more complex.

What is Waste?
D - Defects: Damaged stock that can't be sold or fails to meet expectations
O - Over Production: Stock arriving early or making more than required
W - Waiting: Waiting for instructions, information or equipment
N - Non-Utilised Talent: Not utilising a person's skill, experience, knowledge or creativity
T - Transportation: Unnecessary movement of equipment or stock
I - Inventory: Excess stock in the stockroom taking up valuable space
M - Motion: Walking without a H cage, Z rail or product
E - Extra Processing: Individually pricing boxed product instead of changing the label

## Page 6

Cage Identifiers

These discs are essential to indicate the status of a H cage or Z rail throughout sequence and other store processes.

Overstock
Red disc to be placed on each overstock H cage/Z rail generated from load fill.

Manager Validated
Yellow disc to be placed on each overstock H cage/Z rail that has been validated by a manager.
Validation includes checking for further fill opportunities and stock that has been filled incorrectly. During trials there was a 25% reduction in stock to be packed away due to manager validation.

Back Fill
Green disc to be placed on each backfill H cage/Z rail picked from the stockroom ready to be filled onto the shop floor.

Shopkeeping / New Lines
Blue disc to be placed on each new lines H cage/Z rail during load fill, ready to be actioned by the shopkeeper.

Tagging
Pink disc to be placed on every H cage/Z rail that requires merchandise protection standards.

Online
Orange disc to be placed on every online and click & collect H cage/Z rail.

Cage Identifiers Continued

The cage identifiers are to be set up in a predetermined location for the team to collect before sequence. The discs and carabiners are to be replenished after each sequence.

The cage identifiers are to be held on the carabiners as follows:
• Sequence Team Members – Red, blue, green and pink

Sequence Equipment Cage

Equipment cages are to be placed on the shopfloor to minimise motion waste by keeping essential tools within easy reach, ensuring efficiency and reducing delays for the sequence team.

There should be a maximum of 3 equipment cages clearly identified and placed on the shopfloor ready for sequence in a predetermined location.

(One cage in home, kids and clothing, depending on your store size)

The equipment cages are to be placed on the shopfloor at the beginning of sequence and removed and tidied at the end of sequence by the runner.

The cages should have the following equipment as a minimum:
• Safety equipment
• Label printers
• Tagging equipment
• Stationary
• Hangers & clips
• Additional fittings
• Additional cage identifiers

Note: If there are items needed additional to what is in the equipment cage, the team are to ask the runner over the Push 2 Talk to collect and deliver.

Sequence Roles – Sequence Manager

The sequence manager ensures tasks are completed efficiently, maintains team productivity and drives the overall performance by effective planning, coaching and leading the team.

Before Sequence
• Build the sequence planning tool and allocate the required team members to the tasks with minimal spare time to avoid over rostering.
    • It is recommended that team members are assigned departments where they work independently rather than in groups. This reduces congestion, motion waste, and is easier for the sequence manager to lead load fill.
    • All departments are to be allocated during load fill, this includes books.
• Hold a huddle at the beginning of sequence, covering all the required topics and allocate workload to the team.

During Sequence
• Support the team with regular check in's, this supports to keep the pace of sequence, understand where the team are at, highlights areas of opportunity and to give kudos and feedback.
• Cage identifiers to be used correctly.
• Validate overstock cages and rails to identify further opportunities of fill.
    • Replace the red disc with a yellow disc once validation is complete. Once the yellow disc is in place, this is a signal for the runner to take the H cage/Z rail to zone 3. (pack away)
• Any tasks outside of sequence are to be supported by a line manager.

After Sequence
• Discuss areas of continuous improvement within the process and work with the leadership team to action these ready for the next sequence.

Note: The sequence manager is not to form part of sequence, the role is to lead the process.

• Encourage and motivate the runner after each check in.

## Page 11

Sequence Roles – Sequence Team Member

The sequence team member completes the workload for load fill, pack away and back fill as assigned by the sequence manager following the planning tool.

Each task in sequence is to be completed as a team before moving on to the next.

Sequence Principles
• Sequence team members are to start and finish each task together. Breaks are to be taken together as a team where possible.
• Complete all assigned workload before moving on to the next assigned task.
• Wear a Push 2 Talk to communicate to the sequence manager and runner.
• H Cages and Z rails to be taken down the aisles whilst filling (where possible) to reduce motion waste.
• Shelf edge labels to be printed to the portable printer when required.

Load Fill
• Use the red cage identifier for highlighting when a H cage or Z rail is overstock.
• Complete a visual check of stock on the H cage/Z rail and plan the route of filling to ensure the same location isn't returned to twice.
• Correct filling standards to be followed and shelf edge labels in place for every product.
• Simple new lines are to be cut in and complex new lines are to be actioned by the shopkeeper. New line H cages/Z rails are to be highlighted with a blue cage identifier, ready for the shopkeeper to action.
• Communicate with the runner and manager when the last H cage/Z rail is being worked on, so the next workload can be delivered.

Pack Away
• Pack away overstocks as assigned by the sequence manager.
• Products are to be packed away in the correct location using the pack away function in the PDT.
• Communicate with the runner and manager when the last H cage/Z rail is being worked on, so next workload can be delivered.

Back Fill
• Use the green cage identifier for highlighting when a H cage or Z rail is ready to be filled to the shopfloor.
• Pick back fill fixtures as assigned by the sequence manager.
• Communicate with the runner and manager when the last H cage/Z rail is being picked so equipment can be delivered to continue picking.

## Page 12

Sequence Roles – Runner

The runner is required for all tasks within sequence, this role removes the motion waste from the sequence team.

Runner Principles
• Required for each sequence for the full duration of load fill, back fill and pack away.
• Delivers workload to the sequence team members.
• Communicates and listens to the sequence team members and managers through the Push 2 Talk.
• H Cages and Z rails are placed in each departments designated area out of the main walkways.
• The quantity of H cages/Z rails should not congest the shopfloor or impact the customer experience. Maximum of 2 H cages or Z rails per department during trade.
• Collects empty H cages, Z rails, crates and returns them to the designated area in the stockroom.
• Asks for a boost from the sequence manager when required.
• On completion of each task the shopfloor is to be cleared of H cages, Z rails and equipment cages.

Load Fill
• Returns H cages/Z rails highlighted with a yellow cage identifier (manager validated) to zone 3 (pack away) in the stockroom.
• When returning overstocks to the stockroom record each H cage and Z rail on the output form in preparation for the pack away calculation by the sequence manager.

Pack Away
• Delivers H cages/Z rails from zone 3 to the sequence team to pack away.
• Removes and returns empty H cages/Z rails to the designated area in the stockroom.
• Completes the H cage and Z rail count for overstocks using the output form.

Back Fill
• Delivers empty H cages/Z rails to the sequence team.
• Removes full H cages and Z rails picked for backfill to zone 2 (load fill) ready for fill into the shopfloor.

## Page 13

Sequence Roles - Shopkeeper

The shopkeeper is important to have in place as they complete the tasks outside of sequence. When the shopkeeper is not utilised correctly this can impact store standards.

In principle depending on the store size, shopkeeping can occur at different times. If you are a high or medium volume store, shopkeeping is to run alongside sequence. For low volume stores shopkeeping can run after sequence or on no load days.

• Shopkeeping notes are to be created by the line manager on the shopkeeping template and to be prepared in advance of the team members shift.
    • If the manager is not present the same day, the notes are to be created the day before, ready for the shopkeeper.

• The notes are to be clear, concise and provide meaningful workload. Each task is to have an expected time allocation applied.

• The manager is to complete check ins with the shopkeeper during the shift.

The below is an example of the tasks to be completed by the shopkeeper, they are not limited to this:

• Safety
• Shelf edge labels
• New Lines/Shopkeeping cages from sequence (blue cage identifier)
• End changes
• Layout adjustments
• Visual merchandising
• Priority filling
• Customer service
• Tagging

Note: Shopkeepers are not to form part of sequence load fill, back fill or pack away.

Sequence Huddle Agenda (10-minute huddle)

Sequence Rostering Principles

It is recommended that the rosters for sequence are reviewed with the Store Manager and Zone Manager to ensure they sit in line with the principles.

Sequence
• Teams are to start and finish at the same time. There can be longer and shorter finish times to support with rostering and workload requirements:
    • Longer shift – E.g. 7am start and 3pm finish.
    • Shorter shift – E.g. 7am start and 12pm finish.
• 2 loads (mega sequence) can be processed within the same load fill, both manifests need to be entered into the planning tool to ensure accurate rostering.

Decant
• Decant and load fill of the same load is not to occur at the same time.
• The team are to start and finish together e.g. 5pm-8pm. It is recommended that decant is a 3hr-3.5hr shift for maximised productivity.
• Depending on load size (2-3+ loads) there can be more than 1 decant a day.

Backfill and Pack Away
• Back fill can occur outside of sequence however a manager is to lead the process, understand the workload and finish times using the planning tool and assign the workload to the team.
• If back fill is occurring in sequence, pack away or backfill can be completed first depending on store preference, size and equipment needs.
• Pack away is not to be performed outside of sequence, this should follow on from load fill or back fill.

Huddle
• A huddle is to occur before each task to assign workload, explain finish times and provide kudos and feedback from the previous task.

## Page 21

Load Fill Principles

Load fill is always to be the first task completed during sequence.

Speed to shelf for each load ensures availability for the customer, this improves the customer experience and can increase sales.

• All workload is to be completed by the sequence team and runner.

• Load fill is to be completed before peak trade. (where possible and determined by the workload in the planning tool)

• The runner is to deliver the workload to the sequence team from zone 2 (load fill) and remove empty H cages, Z rails and crates from the shopfloor.

• Zone 2 is to be cleared, and all workload processed before moving on to the next task.

• Correct filling standards to be followed and shelf edge labels in place for every product.

• Simple new lines are to be cut in and complex new lines are to be actioned by the shopkeeper.

Back Fill Principles

Back fill replenishes stock that has sold on the shopfloor from the stockroom fixtures.

It is essential for maintaining product availability on the shopfloor.

• All workload is to be completed by the sequence team and runner.

• A huddle is completed at the beginning of backfill to assign workload and time standards.

• The runner is to deliver empty H cages, Z rails and crates to the sequence team and removes full back fill H cages and Z rails and places into zone 2.

• H Cages and Z rails are to be taken down the aisles where possible or placed at the top of the relevant aisle to reduce motion waste.

• The sequence manager is to make sure the time standards are met and provides coaching and kudos to the team.

## Page 22

Pack Away Principles

Pack away ensures that overstock products are stored in an organised way making it easier to locate in the stockroom and to complete the back fill process.

• All workload is to be completed by the sequence team and runner.

• A huddle is completed at the beginning of pack away to assign workload and provide time standards.

• The runner is to deliver overstock H cages/Z rails to the sequence team and remove empty H cages, Z rails and crates to the designated area in the stockroom.

• H Cages and Z rails are to be taken down the aisles where possible to reduce motion waste.

• The sequence manager is to make sure the time standards are met and provides coaching and kudos to the team.

• Products are to be packed away in the correct location using pack away function on the PDT.

Pack Away Calculation

The pack away output form is used to tally overstocks generated from load fill. It supports to calculate the workload, and the time required to complete pack away.

The output form is to be placed in a visible and easily accessible place for the runner.

• During load fill when the runner returns overstocks to the stockroom, they are to record each H cage or Z rail on the output form in preparation for the sequence manager.

• The below calculation is to be completed by the sequence manager to calculate the workload, apply time standards and assign workload to the sequence team.

• Runner to deliver workload to the team members from zone 2.
• Planning tool to be printed and in use to keep track of progress and pace.

Pack Away
• Calculate and assign the workload and communicate the finish time to the team during the pack away huddle.
• Coach the team to achieve the forecasted finish time of each task.
• Stock to be packed away into fixtures as per the business standard.
• Complete all workload within the forecasted finish time.
• Runner to deliver workload to the team members from zone 3 and remove empty equipment.

Back Fill
• Assign the workload and communicate the finish time to the team during the back fill huddle.
• Coach the team to achieve the forecasted finish time of each task.
• Stock to be picked from fixtures as per the business standard.
• Complete all workload within the forecasted finish time.
• Runner to remove full H cages and Z rails into zone 2 and replace empty equipment.
• Back fill workload to be processed as per load fill principles to the shopfloor.

End of Process
• Shopfloor to be clear of all equipment, H cages and Z rails.
• Complete huddle providing feedback and kudos from sequence.

Sequence Troubleshooting

The below scenarios will help to support during the times things may not go to plan.

We are not finishing sequence on time and not getting to all the tasks required during sequence.
• Make sure the sequence principles in this manual are in place and complete a sequence self health check.
• Has the planning tool been completed correctly?
• Do the team understand the sequence principles, and have they completed the sequence training modules?
• Is there sequence manager providing coaching, keeping the pace and providing feedback and kudos?

My planning tool has too much spare time in the green.
• Review the planning tool to make sure all the cells have the correct information entered.
• Adjust the number of team members required for sequence to avoid over rostering.
    • This may require utilising excess team members in another role.
    • This may require cancelling a casual shift that is not required.

My planning tool has too much time in the red.
• Review the planning tool to make sure all the cells have the correct information entered.
• Adjust the number of team members required for sequence so you can understand the number of team required for the workload.
• With the assistance of the store manager ensure the correct amount of team members are rostered to reflect your stores productivity.
    • This may include reallocating team members within other tasks.
    • This may include adding additional shifts with the approval of your store manager.
    • If the spare time remains in the red all the sequence tasks may not be completed.

There are overstocks that haven't been packed away from the previous day, what should I do?
• Review and understand why the overstocks were not packed away and put a plan together to rectify.
• Count the number of overstock H cages and Z rails to be packed away.
• Put the number into the pack away section in the planning tool and add it to the workload for the day. This can also be applied to additional load fill and backfill H cages and Z rails.
