Policy - Team Member Performance and Behavior

INTRODUCTION AND PURPOSE
Kmart is committed to ensuring that all employees perform and behave in accordance with business expectations based upon their role.

POLICY APPLICATION
This policy applies to all Kmart team members, where an industrial agreement provides a process for addressing team member performance and behavior the terms of the industrial agreement will apply unless reference is made to this policy.

COUNTRY APPLICATION
Australia

POLICY
Where aspects of a team member's performance or behaviour are unsatisfactory, their manager should follow a 'best practice' performance improvement approach which involves:

• identifying how the team member's performance or behaviour is not meeting business expectations and seeking the team member's response;
• establishing and communicating Kmart's expectations and what needs to be done in order to improve the team member's performance or behaviour; and
• communicating the consequences of failing to meet Kmart's expectations.

There are a range of disciplinary and other measures that may flow from unsatisfactory performance or behaviour, depending upon the seriousness and nature of the issue/s in question. Managers should contact their Human Resources Manager or Business Partner for advice and assistance when:

• conducting performance and behaviour improvement meetings or investigations; and
• when devising action plans; and/or
• when issuing disciplinary measures

Managing Performance or Behaviour that does not Meet Business Expectations

Performance relates to a team member's productivity, efficiency and work ethic.

Behaviour relates to how a team member communicates and interacts with colleagues, customers/suppliers and other stakeholders.

Situations where performance would not meet business expectations includes a team member:

• not performing tasks that are required in his/her role;
• not following established procedures;

• not meeting KPIs, deliverables or established performance objectives however described;
• not pursuing Kmart's strategic objectives; or
• making repeated errors.

Situations where behaviour would not meet business expectations includes a team member:

• breaching a policy of Kmart or other workplace law;
not demonstrating the Kmart Values and Behaviours as identified in the Code of Conduct;
• ignoring safety requirements;
• having unexplained or unauthorised absences from work and/or poor punctuality for work;
• harassing, discriminating against or bullying other team members or visitors to the workplace; or
• not meeting the required personal presentation standards.

In managing team member performance or behaviour and assessing the disciplinary action to be taken, the seriousness of the performance or behaviour shortfall must be considered.

A failure to meet business expectations in relation to performance or behaviour may result in a team member being disqualified from being eligible to receive a bonus pursuant to the Kmart Bonus Program.

Managing Serious Performance or Behaviour Breaches (Serious Misconduct)

Situations potentially warranting termination of employment without notice or without having issued prior warnings, include where a team member:

• Engages in any serious or wilful behaviour that is inconsistent with the continuation of the contract of employment;
• Wilfully disobeying or disregarding a reasonable and lawful direction given to you by Kmart;
• Committing any act of dishonesty or fraud whether in relation to Kmart, its employees or otherwise;
• Engaging in any conduct or behaviour that causes serious and imminent risk to the health and safety of yourself, another team member or customer;
• Committing any act which in Kmart's reasonable opinion is likely to seriously injure the Company's good standing, reputation or business; or;
• Being convicted for an offence that precludes or inhibits the further performance of your duties.

Where the behaviour of a team member has resulted in termination of employment and that behaviour may constitute criminal behaviour, managers should consult with Human Resources to determine if and when the matter should be referred to police.
